Research · hidden costs

Filtering changes the compute equation

An ICLR 2026 study finds that deduplication, heuristic filtering, and LLM-guided rewriting can shift compute-optimal token-to-parameter ratios by orders of magnitude. Curation is part of the training strategy: it changes which data and compute budget make sense.

Why this matters: Measure the curation pipeline and training run together; “dataset size” alone can hide the real cost-performance tradeoff.

Myth buster · repetition

“Higher quality” can still mean worse returns

An ICML 2026 paper reports that upweighting high-quality data may hurt in data-limited, overtrained regimes when it causes excessive repetition.

Why this matters: Track repeats and validation learning curves when fresh data is scarce.

01 / UNDERDOG

Small data can be a smart baseline

An ICML 2026 study finds that repeating a smaller dataset can speed learning in some settings. It is a conditional result, not a universal recipe.

Why this matters: Compare a well-chosen small set against the cost of sourcing and processing more examples.

02 / PRODUCTION LESSON

The data loader is part of the attack surface

Hugging Face’s 2026 intrusion timeline describes abuse of dataset-processing paths, including unsafe handling of HDF5 external references and template expressions in data fields.

Why this matters: Treat training data and configs as untrusted input, and isolate processing workers.

After review · practical direction

Make the data mix measurable and revisable

Recent research does not establish one universally best dataset size. It points instead to a task- and budget-dependent choice among data quality, coverage, repetition, and compute. As a practical inference, compare data mixtures on held-out evaluations and record the marginal gain per added unit of data and processing cost.

Why this matters: A decision log lets the next training run build on measured results instead of repeating a scale-first assumption.
